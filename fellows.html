Responsible GeoAI Fellows

A paid fellowship for UT Austin undergraduates who want to take a strong project on maps, geospatial data or AI one step further, and present it in public.

Up to 3Fellows each round
Up to $500per Fellow
Spring 2027first round

Why

Maps, geospatial data and AI now shape decisions about housing, health care and public services. A course project can already show where a dataset leaves people out or where a model gets a place wrong. The fellowship gives students the time, mentoring and audience to carry that work forward, with Prof. Yuhao Kang's GISense Lab.

What Fellows do

Over about six weeks, each Fellow takes their project one concrete step further: improve the analysis, add new data, build a story map or dashboard, or write a short brief for a city or community partner. Fellows then present the work in person at a public showcase: Demo Day in spring rounds, or UT GIS Day in fall rounds. The award is paid after the presentation.

Who can apply

  • Students from a participating course with a strong final project, for example GRG 356T Spatial Data Science and Maps.
  • Open applicants: any UT Austin undergraduate with an existing project that uses geospatial data, maps or AI on a civic question.

Applicants must be enrolled at UT Austin through the showcase date. Team projects are welcome; each team member applies individually. Beginners in coding are welcome too.

How to apply

The application is a short online form (UT login). It asks for your project (a link or a PDF) and three short answers of up to 150 words each:

  1. The civic question. What question does your project answer, and who in Austin, or in your community, could use the answer?
  2. Responsible GeoAI. Where could your data, model or map mislead people or treat some groups unfairly? What did you do, or what will you do, about it?
  3. Your fellowship plan. In about six weeks, how will you take the project one step further?

How we select

Two reviewers score each application independently against six criteria.

CriterionWhat we look forWeight
Project qualityComplete, sound work; at best, an outside audience could use it as is25%
Responsible GeoAI practiceNames a specific risk in the data, model or map, and shows a check or fix20%
Civic relevanceA clear civic question with a real user: a city, community or campus partner20%
Plan feasibilityA concrete, well-scoped step that fits six weeks and ends in a public output15%
CommunicationClear, specific writing that non-experts can follow10%
Technical skillsAble to carry out the plan with light mentoring10%
